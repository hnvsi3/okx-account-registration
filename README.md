## How OKX trading fees work

The most important distinction is between **spot trading** and simplified buy or convert functions.

### Spot trading fees

In spot trading, orders are placed against an order book. A market order typically takes available liquidity and is charged according to the taker rate. A limit order may add liquidity to the order book and can qualify for the maker rate if it is filled under the applicable conditions.

OKX explains the basic calculation as:

> Trading fee = applicable fee rate × the amount of crypto bought or sold when the order is filled.

The exact rate depends on the instrument, your fee tier, and other account or market conditions. OKX advises users to check the fee information in the order-placement panel before submitting an order.

A simple example:

- You buy $1,000 of crypto.
- Your applicable taker fee is 0.10%.
- The trading fee is approximately $1 before considering price movement or other charges.

That example is only arithmetic, not a promise about the current rate on a specific OKX pair. Your own fee panel is the relevant source at the time you trade.

### Simple Buy and Sell

Simple Buy and Sell is designed for convenience. You receive a quoted price for a purchase or sale rather than placing an order directly on the order book.

OKX says the quote can include a spread, which means the purchase and sale prices may differ even when no separate trading-fee line appears.

This route may make sense when:

- You are placing a small, straightforward purchase.
- You do not need advanced order controls.
- You want to see an all-in quote before confirming.
- You prefer a simpler interface.

Before confirming, compare the quoted amount received with the amount you expect. “No separate fee” does not mean the transaction has no cost. The spread is part of the pricing.

### Convert

Convert allows you to exchange one supported asset for another. It can be convenient for moving between assets without using the full spot-trading interface.

The same rule applies: inspect the quote, the amount received, and any displayed spread before confirming. If you are making a larger conversion, compare it with the order-book price instead of assuming the quick route is automatically cheaper.

### Deposit and withdrawal costs

OKX states that crypto deposits do not require an OKX deposit fee. Withdrawals are different: the sending party pays the withdrawal fee, and the amount can vary by network and asset.

Before sending crypto to OKX, check:

- The deposit address
- The supported network
- The minimum deposit amount
- Whether a memo or tag is required
- Whether the asset and network combination match exactly

Sending an asset over the wrong network can create recovery problems. A low advertised fee is not useful if the funds are sent through an unsupported route.

## How to make your first crypto purchase

If you are new to crypto, keep the first transaction small enough that a wrong address, unfamiliar interface, or temporary price movement will not create a serious financial problem.

A cautious first workflow is:

1. Complete identity verification.
2. Review the deposit or payment method.
3. Deposit a small amount.
4. Compare Simple Buy with spot trading.
5. Review the quoted price and total amount received.
6. Confirm the order only after checking the asset and currency.
7. Review the completed transaction in your order history.
8. Test a small withdrawal only after understanding the network and fee.

For an external crypto deposit, copy the address and verify the first and last characters on both screens. For a withdrawal, confirm the destination network separately from the wallet address. These checks take seconds and can prevent expensive mistakes.

Avoid using leverage or derivatives simply because they appear in educational material or social media promotions. Spot trading and buying crypto already involve price risk. Leveraged products introduce liquidation and funding mechanics that are inappropriate for many first-time account holders.
